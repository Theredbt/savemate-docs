HLS (HTTP Live Streaming) is the dominant adaptive streaming format on the web today. News sites, educational platforms, sports broadcasters, and corporate video portals all rely on it. Video Downloader Plus functions as a built-in m3u8 downloader inside Chrome — no desktop app, no command-line tools required.

## What is HLS?

HLS breaks a video into short segments (typically 2–10 seconds each) stored as `.ts` files, then uses an M3U8 playlist file to tell the player which segment to fetch next. This design enables adaptive bitrate switching: the player silently upgrades or degrades quality based on your connection.

For a deeper technical breakdown, see the [HLS glossary entry](/glossary/hls).

## How to download an HLS stream

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the page">
    Navigate to any site delivering video through HLS. This covers most news publishers, course platforms, sports streaming sites, and video CDNs.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Start playback">
    Press play on the video. Many platforms use lazy initialization — the M3U8 manifest is only requested once the player activates. Starting playback triggers the network request that Video Downloader Plus intercepts.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open Video Downloader Plus">
    Click the Video Downloader Plus icon in your Chrome toolbar. When streams are present, the icon badge lights up with a count. The popup lists every detected stream along with quality variants and estimated file sizes.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick a quality level">
    HLS streams typically offer several renditions — 360p, 480p, 720p, 1080p, and sometimes 4K. Select the quality that suits your storage and playback needs.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Start the download">
    Click **Download**. Video Downloader Plus fetches all segments in parallel, then assembles them into a single output file. Progress is shown in the popup.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Converting HLS to MP4

When you download an HLS stream in its native format, you get a `.ts` container. This plays in VLC and most media players, but some devices — particularly mobile ones — handle it poorly. Video Downloader Plus's Professional plan includes in-browser format conversion, so you can target the container that fits your workflow.

| Format | Best for |
|--------|----------|
| **MP4** | Universal playback across every device and media player |
| **MKV** | Preserving multiple audio tracks, subtitle streams, or chapters |
| **MOV** | Apple ecosystem apps and professional video editing on macOS |
| **WebM** | Web embedding, open-source pipelines, and smaller file sizes |

Conversion runs entirely in your browser using WebAssembly — no files are transmitted to any server during the process.

<Info>Format conversion is a [Professional feature](/billing/plans). On the free plan, HLS streams download in their native `.ts` format. VLC Media Player opens `.ts` files without any configuration.</Info>

## Separate audio and video tracks

Newer HLS streams commonly split audio and video into separate media playlists — a technique called demuxed audio. This allows broadcasters to serve multiple audio languages from a single video track. Video Downloader Plus detects both the video rendition and the matching audio rendition, downloads them simultaneously, and muxes them into one file. You do not need to merge tracks manually.

## Tips for better HLS downloads

- **Refresh and play if detection misses** — If the Video Downloader Plus badge does not appear, reload the page and press play before checking again. Some players defer the manifest request until user interaction.
- **Compare quality options** — Video Downloader Plus often exposes more quality levels than the site's native player interface shows. Check the popup for hidden higher-resolution options.
- **Plan storage before long downloads** — A two-hour stream at 1080p can reach 4 GB or more. The popup displays an estimated file size so you can verify available disk space before committing.
- **Use file size previews** — For m3u8 video downloader use cases where bandwidth matters (mobile hotspot, metered connections), the size estimate helps you decide whether to step down to 720p or 480p.
