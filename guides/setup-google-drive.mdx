Google Drive connects through OAuth 2.0, which means Vidow never sees your Google password. You authorize access once, and Google issues a token that Vidow uses for uploads.

## Prerequisites

- A Vidow account on the [Professional plan](/billing/plans)
- A Google account with sufficient Drive storage for your videos

## Connect Google Drive

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Cloud Sync">
    Go to [vidow.io/cloud-sync](https://vidow.io/cloud-sync) and sign in to your Vidow account.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click Connect on Google Drive">
    Locate the Google Drive card and click **Connect**. Vidow redirects you to Google's OAuth authorization page.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose your Google account">
    Pick the Google account you want to use. If several accounts are listed, select the one with enough free space for video storage.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Grant permissions">
    Google asks you to confirm that Vidow can manage files in your Drive. Vidow writes only to a dedicated folder it creates — your existing files and folders remain untouched.

    Click **Allow** to complete the authorization.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connection confirmed">
    You're returned to Vidow. The Google Drive card now displays a green **Connected** indicator, and you can start syncing videos.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## How uploads work

Vidow uses Google's **resumable upload** protocol, which is designed to handle large files reliably:

- Files smaller than 100MB are sent in a single HTTP request
- Larger files open a resumable session, which tolerates network interruptions
- If a transfer drops partway through, it picks up from the last confirmed byte rather than restarting
- All uploads are chunked into 5MB segments to keep memory usage predictable

## Storage location

Vidow creates a dedicated folder at the root of your Google Drive. Every synced video lands in that folder. You can move or rename files inside Drive after the upload without affecting Vidow's ability to write new ones.

## Managing the connection

### Connection health

The Cloud Sync page shows a live status for each connected provider:

- **Healthy** — The OAuth token is current and uploads are working normally
- **Broken** — The token was invalidated or expired. Click **Reconnect** to go through authorization again
- **Revoked** — You removed Vidow's access from your Google account. Reconnect from the Cloud Sync page to restore it

<Tip>Vidow refreshes OAuth tokens automatically before they expire, so you should rarely see a Broken status during normal use.</Tip>

### Disconnect

To remove the Google Drive connection from Vidow:

1. Visit [vidow.io/cloud-sync](https://vidow.io/cloud-sync)
2. Open the options on the Google Drive card and choose **Disconnect**
3. The connection is deleted from Vidow — no files in Drive are affected

To also cut off access from Google's side, visit [myaccount.google.com/permissions](https://myaccount.google.com/permissions) and remove Vidow from the list of connected apps.

## Troubleshooting

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Upload fails with a permission error">
    The most likely cause is an expired or revoked OAuth token. Go to [vidow.io/cloud-sync](https://vidow.io/cloud-sync), disconnect Google Drive, then reconnect and reauthorize. New tokens are issued during the reconnect flow.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Storage quota exceeded">
    Check how much space is available at [drive.google.com/settings](https://drive.google.com/settings). Free Google accounts share 15GB across Drive, Gmail, and Google Photos. If you're running low, free up space in Drive or consider upgrading your Google One storage plan.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Connection shows as broken">
    Google can invalidate a token for several reasons:

    - You changed your Google account password
    - You manually removed Vidow's access in your Google account settings
    - Google flagged unusual activity on your account

    In any of these cases, click **Reconnect** on the Cloud Sync page to issue a fresh token.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
